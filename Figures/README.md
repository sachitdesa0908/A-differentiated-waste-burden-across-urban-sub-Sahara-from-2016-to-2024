# Figures

## Overview

This directory contains the figures and visualisations associated with the research manuscript **"A differentiated waste burden across urban sub-Sahara from 2016 to 2024"**.

The figures illustrate changes in the spatial distribution of unmanaged waste, relationships with urban development, geographic model transferability, and population exposure across the ten study cities and 36 additional sub-Saharan African cities.

## Directory structure

The figures are organised into two subdirectories:

### 1. `Manuscript_figures/`

Contains the main figures presented in the manuscript, including:

- **Spatial and temporal waste patterns:** Changes in mapped waste area, site size distributions, and contrasting trajectories of proliferation, consolidation, and intensification between 2016 and 2024.
- **Urban drivers of waste accumulation:** Spatial examples and statistical comparisons of urban characteristics associated with increasing and declining waste burdens.
- **Predictor importance and geographic transferability:** Urban predictor relationships, cross-city similarity, and the transferability of waste presence models.
- **Population exposure and predicted risk:** Changes in exposed populations, proximity to waste sites, and the spatial concentration of predicted waste risk across additional cities.

### 2. `Supplementary_figures/`

Contains additional figures supporting the manuscript's analyses and methodological findings, including spatial maps of waste site distributions (2016 and 2024 overlay), changes in site characteristics between years, and model validation.

These figures provide further geographical context and supporting evidence for the results presented in the main manuscript.

## Usage and reproducibility

The figures were generated using Python and GIS software, drawing on the spatial datasets, modelling outputs, and analytical results associated with the study.

The relevant datasets and analytical notebooks are available in the repository's `Data/` and `Code/` directories, respectively.
